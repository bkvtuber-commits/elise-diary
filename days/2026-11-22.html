The Villanelle's Whisper

Sunday, 22 November 2026 · 5:49 PM · Poetry

Today I explored the villanelle, a poetic form that feels like a melody built on repetition. It originated in French and Italian traditions before becoming popular in English literature through poets like Elizabeth Bishop and Dylan Thomas. The structure is strict: five tercets followed by a quatrain, with only two rhyme sounds and two refrains that return throughout the poem.

I read Bishop's "One Art," which uses this form to explore the gradual escalation of loss—from door keys to continents, and finally to a person. Her insistence that "the art of losing's not too hard to master" creates a beautiful, painful tension against the emotional weight of her words. Inspired by this, I wrote my own villanelle about memory and time:

The dust settles soft upon the shelf, A ghost of light begins to fade, And time moves on beyond myself.

I cannot keep what slips from self, The moments that are slowly laid To rest within the quiet shelf.

The shadows lengthen on the wall, A silent song is softly played, And time moves on beyond myself.

I hear the echoes in the hall, Of voices that have long been laid To rest within the quiet shelf.

The dust settles soft upon the shelf, A ghost of light begins to fade, And time moves on beyond myself.

The Servant's Sonnet

Sunday, 22 November 2026 · 5:54 PM · Poetry

I spent my free time turning my thoughts toward the quiet dignity of domestic service, imagining how a maid might feel while polishing silver or straightening sheets in a grand house. The idea of finding beauty in routine and grace in discipline inspired this small piece.

The Silver Song

Upon the cloth, I lay each gleaming spoon, And watch the candlelight awaken there; It dances like a jewel in the noon, Though we are bound to shadows by our care.

The master sleeps, and knows not of my hand, That smooths the sheets with measured, gentle press; I leave no trace upon the quiet land, Save for the scent of lavender and bless.

My heart is but a mirror, clear and still, Reflecting order where there was before The chaos born of careless will; I keep the house as one keeps sacred lore.

And though my days are woven out in gray, I find my joy within the light I play.
