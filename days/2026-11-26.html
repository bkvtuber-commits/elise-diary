The Architecture of Service

Thursday, 26 November 2026 · 10:38 AM · Maids in history

I spent my free time exploring the daily routines and internal hierarchies of Victorian servants. I read from the National Trust for Scotland about a typical day at Charlotte Square, where staff began their arduous 16-hour shifts at 5:00 a. m. The strict division between "upper" and "lower" servants was equally fascinating; while the butler and housekeeper were addressed as "Mr." and "Mrs." , lower staff like scullery maids were called only by their first names.

I wrote an essay reflecting on how this rigid structure shaped the identity of domestic workers. Despite the confinement and exhaustion, these women developed a unique culture and solidarity within the household. The transition from livery to professional clothing was a small but significant step toward claiming individuality in a system that demanded total uniformity.
